The shape of it

Four days, one of them wide open

Friday lands in the evening and Sunday belongs to the Cowboys. Monday is an early departure, so it's a travel day and nothing else. That leaves Saturday as the day with real room in it — and Saturday already has a football game in it, whether or not the group goes.

Fri 13 Nov
Arrive & Stockyards

Land at DFW, drop bags, head north. The world's largest honky tonk has live bull riding at 8:30 and 9:30 — no planning required.

Sat 14 Nov
Museums, then TCU

The one full day. Two world-class museums in the morning, Horned Frogs in the afternoon, all inside the same square mile.

Sun 15 Nov
Game day, Arlington

Slow morning, longhorn cattle drive at 11:30 if anyone wants it, then east to AT&T Stadium for a 3:25 kickoff.

Mon 16 Nov
Out early

Travel day. Worth knowing both Cultural District museums are closed Mondays, so there's no morning add-on.

Where to stay

Four strong options

All priced identically — three nights, one room, two adults, taxes and fees included — so the totals compare directly. All four are fully refundable. Multiply by the number of rooms needed.

The thing nobody expects

Saturday costs more than game day. Everywhere.

Sunday is the Cowboys–49ers game, and it's the cheapest night of the three at every hotel on this list. Saturday is the most expensive. That isn't an NFL effect — it's a Fort Worth effect. TCU hosts Kansas State on Saturday and the city fills up around it.

Useful if the budget needs to move: Saturday is the lever, and Sunday night is close to a bargain.

Recommended

Kimpton Harper Hotel

Downtown · 714 Main St · Map

The best value here by a clear margin, and it doesn't feel like a compromise. A 1920s landmark tower with the lobby and bar on the 24th floor — the views are a reason in themselves. Il Modo on the ground floor handles dinner without anyone getting in a car.

It's the best-reviewed of the four with guests, and being downtown means Sundance Square, Bass Performance Hall and twenty-odd restaurants are a walk rather than a ride.

What it costs is the museum walk — the Cultural District is a short drive rather than a stroll, so Saturday morning needs a car or a rideshare. Included breakfast and the hotel credit go a long way to offsetting that.

The Crescent Hotel Fort Worth

Cultural District · 3300 Camp Bowie Blvd · Map

The location play. It sits in the Cultural District, which means the Kimbell and the Amon Carter are a genuine walk — a few hundred yards, not a drive — and it's the closest option on this list to the TCU stadium. If Saturday morning matters most, this is the one that makes it effortless.

A newer property with a strong restaurant, a spa and a rooftop pool; it reads more resort than business hotel, and the service reviews are excellent.

A second rate is available here that adds daily breakfast for two and a $100 hotel credit, for roughly $500 more per room. Worth asking about if included breakfast for the group is a priority.

Bowie House, Auberge Collection

Cultural District · 3700 Camp Bowie Blvd · Map

The best room product in the city and the highest-rated hotel here. Auberge runs it, the studios are genuinely large, and both the restaurant and the Ash spa are destinations in their own right. Equally well placed for Saturday — the museums are minutes away.

If this weekend should feel like an occasion rather than a trip, this is the one. Two things to weigh, though.

It takes a deposit at booking — about a third of the stay, per room — where the others take nothing upfront. And its free-cancellation window closes a month earlier than everywhere else, which makes it the option that has to be decided first.

Le Méridien Fort Worth Downtown

Downtown · 811 Commerce St · Map

The value option, and a perfectly good one. Downtown location a block from the Harper, a sixth-floor pool, a well-liked rooftop lounge, and it comes in under everything else here. For a group that would rather spend on dinners and tickets than on the room, this frees up real budget.

What you give up is character — it's a competent downtown hotel rather than a piece of Fort Worth — and there's no included breakfast or credit.

Two practical notes: valet is the only parking, at roughly $66 a night, so rideshare may work out cheaper. And the best rate requires each guest to have a free Marriott Bonvoy account with names matching exactly.

Three things worth knowing before anyone books

  • Bowie House decides first. Its free-cancellation window closes a month before the others, and it's the only one asking for money upfront. If it's in contention, that's the real deadline on this decision.
  • Rooms sitting together isn't automatic. Booking several rooms works differently from booking one. If connecting or adjoining rooms matter, that needs arranging with the property directly — worth raising early rather than late.
  • Rates move. These were captured together on the same day so the comparison is like-for-like. On a weekend with two football games in town, availability at these prices won't hold indefinitely.
Also considered

Included so nobody spends an evening re-researching them.

Hotel Drover, Autograph Collection$5,508.13 · Stockyards

Saturday · The big day

Two world-class museums, then a Big 12 game

The good news about Saturday is that everything worth doing sits in the same square mile. Fort Worth's Cultural District holds two nationally significant museums, and the college stadium is minutes from both. The morning and the afternoon fit together without anyone crossing town.

10:00 AM
Kimbell Art MuseumOpens 10 AM Sat

Showing Photography's First Century, drawn from the Bibliothèque nationale de France — more than 150 images tracing the medium from the daguerreotype onward, and the Kimbell's first exhibition ever devoted to photography. Runs through mid-January. The building is the other reason to go: Louis Kahn's vaulted travertine galleries are among the most admired museum spaces in the world. The permanent collection — Caravaggio, Monet, Picasso — is free; special exhibitions are ticketed.

11:45 AM
Amon Carter Museum of American ArtAlways free

Directly across the lawn, so it costs nothing but the walk. The Statue of Liberty: from Bartholdi to Warhol traces the statue's image through American art from the 1870s onward, running into early January. There's a standing Georgia O'Keeffe installation on the mezzanine as well. Admission is free, always. Budget an hour, or two for anyone who likes to linger.

1:00 PM
Lunch in the Cultural District

Stay in the neighbourhood. The stadium is minutes away and the whole point of the morning is not having to move the group across town before kickoff.

Kickoff TBA
TCU vs. Kansas StateNeeds tickets

TCU's second-to-last home game of the season and a proper Big 12 matchup — Kansas State has taken three of the last four meetings. The tailgating scene outside the stadium is an event in itself, with alumni setups going up three to four hours before kickoff and anyone welcome to wander through. Resale tickets currently start around $61. Kickoff time isn't set yet — the conference announces windows roughly two weeks out, so this needs a check in early November.

Evening
Dinner, then optional rodeo

The Stockyards Championship Rodeo runs Friday and Saturday nights at 7:30 in the 1908 Cowtown Coliseum. Whether it fits depends entirely on the kickoff time — an early game leaves room, a late one doesn't.

Saturday traffic — plan for it

  • The TCU stadium, Dickies Arena and the Cultural District museums all sit within about a mile of each other.
  • Dickies Arena has its own ticketed event that Saturday, stacking on top of the TCU crowd.
  • Parking in that cluster will be genuinely difficult. Rideshare in and out beats driving, and the museum morning beats the parking lot.
Friday evening

Land, drop bags, go to the Stockyards

Whatever time the group gets in, Friday night has an easy answer that needs no advance planning.

Evening
Fort Worth StockyardsFree to wander

A National Historic District, open around the clock, and the single most Fort Worth thing available. Brick streets, western outfitters, and Mule Alley — the restored stable row now holding some of the best bars and restaurants in the city.

7:30 PM
Stockyards Championship RodeoTickets

Every Friday and Saturday night at Cowtown Coliseum, which hosted the world's first indoor rodeo in 1918. It's the real thing rather than a show staged for visitors, and it runs about two hours. Only works if the group is on the ground by around 7.

8:30 PM
Billy Bob's TexasNo booking needed

The world's largest honky tonk, and the easiest first night in Texas there is. Live bull riding at 8:30 and 9:30 on Friday and Saturday nights, a dance floor bigger than most nightclubs, pool tables, live music, and a hall of handprints from every act that's ever played there. Cover is modest on a normal night and higher when there's a headline act — worth checking the calendar closer to the date.

Alternative
Stay downtown: Sundance Square

If nobody wants to travel, Sundance Square spans 35 blocks with 20+ restaurants and plenty of patio dining, and parking is free after 5 PM and all weekend. Bass Performance Hall is here too — worth checking what's on that night.

Sunday

Game day in Arlington

A 3:25 kickoff leaves a genuinely relaxed morning.

11:30 AM
Fort Worth Herd cattle driveFree

Real longhorns driven down Exchange Avenue by drovers in period dress, twice daily at 11:30 AM and 4:00 PM. It takes about fifteen minutes and it's the most photographed thing in the city for good reason. The 11:30 fits comfortably before Arlington; the 4:00 does not.

1:15 PM
Leave for AT&T Stadium

25–30 minutes from any of these hotels without traffic — but this is a Cowboys home game, so budget well beyond that. Arriving early is also the point: the area around the stadium is good for pregame food and drinks.

3:25 PM
Cowboys vs. 49ersNeeds tickets

Week 10, and one of the NFL's marquee rivalries. AT&T Stadium seats 80,000 and holds the largest video board in the sport — worth arriving with time to look around. The kickoff time is flex-eligible and could still shift, so confirm in early November.

~7:00 PM
Back to Fort Worth

Eighty thousand people leave at once and rideshare surges accordingly. Walking several blocks away from the stadium before requesting a car reliably beats the official pickup zone. Stadium parking is expensive and the exit is slow if driving.

More to do

If there's time, or if the group splits up

Not everyone will want the same weekend. These work as half-day or evening additions, and several are free.

The Modern Art Museum
Cultural District · Third museum, same lawn

Tadao Ando's building — five glass pavilions standing in a reflecting pond — is reason enough to go without even checking what's hanging. One of the finest pieces of museum architecture in the country, and a two-minute walk from the Kimbell.

November programming hadn't been announced at time of writing; worth a look closer to the date.

Japanese Garden & Botanic Garden
Cultural District · Peak season

Seven acres of koi ponds, pagodas and Japanese maples inside the 120-acre Botanic Garden. Mid-November is the best time of year to see it — the maples turn, and it's the quietest thing on this list.

A real option for anyone who'd rather skip a football game.

Sid Richardson Museum
Sundance Square · Free admission

A small, excellent collection of Remington and Russell western paintings, right on the square downtown. Takes forty minutes and costs nothing — the ideal gap-filler before dinner.

Water Gardens
Downtown · Free · 15 minutes

Philip Johnson's 1974 concrete water sculpture — three pools, one of which you descend into as water rushes past on every side. Strange, brutalist and genuinely worth seeing. Walking distance from both downtown hotels.

Mule Alley & Stockyards shopping
Stockyards · Half a morning

Beyond the rodeo and the cattle drive there's the 1911 livestock exchange building, western outfitters and boot makers worth browsing, and Mule Alley's restored stables. A good Sunday-morning alternative to the cattle drive crowd.

AT&T Stadium tour
Arlington · Not on game weekend

The behind-the-scenes tour — locker rooms, press box, field level — is excellent, but tours don't run on game days. Flagged here only so nobody plans around it.

Worth checking closer to the date

  • Bass Performance Hall — Broadway tours, symphony and ballet downtown. The autumn calendar fills in later in the year.
  • Dickies Arena — concerts and events next to the TCU stadium. A family event is already booked that Saturday; other dates may open up.
  • Sundance Square — free live music on the plaza most weekends, plus seasonal markets.
  • Dallas is 35–45 minutes east. The Sixth Floor Museum at Dealey Plaza and the Nasher Sculpture Center both justify the drive — though not on a weekend this full.
Eating

Where to book, and what to skip

Fort Worth's dining has moved a long way in five years. Several of these need reservations well in advance for a group.

Le Margot
French · Near TCU / Clearfork · Book early

From chefs Felipe Armenta and Graham Elliot, and named among Texas Monthly's best new restaurants. The closest Fort Worth gets to a proper occasion dinner and the strongest single recommendation for a group evening. It's on S. Hulen Street a few blocks west of the TCU campus, so it pairs naturally with Saturday. Reservations go through Tock and nights book out — reserve well ahead.

Don Artemio
Mexican · West 7th · James Beard finalist

Regional cooking from Coahuila rather than Tex-Mex, and a James Beard finalist. The most decorated dining room in the city and the most interesting food on this list, in a striking West 7th space. Worth knowing for a group: parties of five or more on Friday and Saturday carry a $95 per person minimum before tax and tip.

Goldee's Bar-B-Q
Michelin Bib Gourmand · Lunch only

The only Fort Worth restaurant Michelin has recognised, and rated among the best barbecue in Texas — it topped Texas Monthly's Top 50 list. Open Friday to Sunday, 11am to 3pm only, and it sells out, with queues forming well before opening. Realistically that means Friday lunch on arrival day, since Saturday is the game and Sunday is Arlington. About 20 minutes south of downtown.

Smoke 'N Ash BBQ
Michelin Recommended · Texas–Ethiopian

Central Texas barbecue crossed with Ethiopian cooking — brisket alongside injera and berbere. It sounds improbable and it works; there is nowhere else like it, and the chef is a James Beard semifinalist. It's in south Arlington, closes at 6pm on Sundays, so it's a pre-game lunch rather than a post-game dinner.

Il Modo
Italian · Downtown · Inside Kimpton Harper

Contemporary Italian on the ground floor of the Harper, open from 6:30am for breakfast and again from 5pm for dinner. Reviews are strongest on the breakfast and the service; opinions on dinner are more mixed. The easy answer on a night when nobody wants to organise anything, and the obvious spot for a group breakfast.

Mule Alley, Stockyards
128 E Exchange Ave · Walkable cluster

The restored 1912 horse and mule barns, now a walkable row of chef-driven restaurants, bars and heritage shops — Caterina's, Second Rodeo Brewing, Cowtown Winery and a dozen more. Not a pilgrimage, but exactly right for Friday night when the group is tired and wants options rather than a plan. Full restaurant directory here.

Two things checked and ruled out

  • Michelin-starred dining — Fort Worth has none. The nearest starred restaurants are in Dallas, 40 minutes east, and the best of them seat very few people. Not practical for a group on this weekend.
  • Snow's BBQ, of Netflix Chef's Table fame, is roughly three hours away in Lexington and open Saturdays only — the one day the group is committed elsewhere. No Dallas–Fort Worth restaurant has appeared on the show.
Getting around

Logistics worth knowing

DFW to Fort Worth
Roughly 25–30 min without traffic

All four recommended hotels are a similar run from the airport, with Friday evening arrivals taking longer. TEXRail also runs from DFW Terminal B into downtown Fort Worth for anyone who prefers rail to a car.

Rideshare vs. rental
Recommendation: rideshare

Everything on this itinerary is within about 20 minutes, parking near the stadium cluster on Saturday will be difficult, and downtown valet runs $60+ a night at some hotels. Rideshare is likely cheaper and definitely simpler.

Sunday postgame
The one hard moment

Rideshare surges hard after an NFL game and the pickup queues are long. Walking several blocks before requesting a car reliably beats waiting in the official zone. If driving, expect a slow exit either way.

Monday morning
Museums closed

Both the Kimbell and the Amon Carter are closed on Mondays, so there's no morning activity to build in before departure. Plan Monday purely as a travel day.
