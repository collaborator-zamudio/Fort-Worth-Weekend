White Elephant Saloon

A Stockyards institution since 1976. Downstairs runs to 2am Sunday; upstairs has live music 7–11pm. Kitchen stops at 9pm, so eat first.

The story
On 8 February 1887, Luke Short, who ran the gambling concession here, shot and killed former city marshal Jim Courtright outside the original White Elephant. It became one of the most retold gunfights in Texas. The saloon moved to this spot on Exchange Avenue in 1976, and the hat collection above the bar belongs to regulars, living and otherwise.

Worth knowing · 1875

A Dallas lawyer wrote to his local paper that Fort Worth was so quiet he had seen a panther asleep in the street. The town took the insult, adopted the panther, and the Fort Worth Democrat engraved a new masthead: “Where the Panther Laid Down.” You’ll see panthers on badges and buildings all weekend. No documentation of the actual panther has ever surfaced.

Worth knowing · Cowtown

Between 1866 and 1890 more than four million head of cattle were driven up the Chisholm Trail straight through here — the last supply stop before the Red River. That is why it is called Cowtown, and why the Stockyards sit exactly where they sit.

One last thing

The margarita story

On 11 May 1971, a Dallas restaurateur named Mariano Martinez got tired of his bartenders making inconsistent margaritas by hand. He bought a soft-serve ice cream machine, filled it with his mother’s recipe, and invented the frozen margarita machine.

He never patented it. The original now sits in the Smithsonian’s National Museum of American History. It was built twenty minutes from where you’ll be sitting on Sunday afternoon.
