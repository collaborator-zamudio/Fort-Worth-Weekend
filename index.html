The Basis

How every number here was arrived at

Nine properties priced, one room and two adults throughout so the totals compare directly. Every figure is the all-in total including taxes and fees, on a rate that can still be cancelled. Every distance is a Google Maps lookup from the property’s own coordinates, not an estimate.

Both stay lengths are priced on every hotel below. Each one leads with its two-night total for Saturday to Monday, with the three-night Friday-to-Monday figure directly underneath — so whichever arrival a couple settles on, the comparison holds without anything needing to be repriced.

The pattern

Saturday is the expensive night in Fort Worth. In Arlington it is Sunday.

Fort Worth empties out on Sunday. Saturday night runs 45–73% higher than Sunday at every Fort Worth property priced — that is the TCU home game filling the city, not the NFL. Arlington does the exact opposite: at the hotel you can walk to the stadium from, Sunday is the most expensive night of the weekend. Grapevine, sitting outside both, is close to flat.

The practical consequence, for anyone weighing a Friday arrival against a Saturday one: dropping Friday takes 37–45% off the total at the Fort Worth hotels, but under 6% at the Arlington one. The extra night is far cheaper in Fort Worth than the headline rates suggest.

Two things to know before comparing prices

  • Every total was checked against booking the hotel directly. Result: eight of the nine match the hotel’s own website, several to the exact cent. Where there is a difference it is noted on that hotel’s entry. Nobody needs to go and price-check this themselves.
  • Two properties will not sell a Saturday–Monday stay at all. The Crescent and Hyatt Place Arlington both return a minimum-night requirement for those dates. That is a planning constraint, not a footnote — it is covered on each entry below.

Game day — Sunday at AT&T Stadium

The Miller Lite HouseFree with a game ticket

The strongest find here and it costs nothing extra. Opens four hours before kickoff, so around 11:25am for a 3:25 start, inside the ticketed perimeter. 87,000 square feet with a 70-yard turf field for tailgate games, 50+ TVs, two beer gardens, live music and $5 beers. Two operators run turnkey tailgates at or beside the stadium — one on Collins Street directly opposite, about three hours pre-kick with a Texas BBQ buffet and Cowboys alumni; another on official lots with setup and clean-up included. Worth knowing before ordering a drink

The frozen margarita was invented twenty minutes from the stadium

On 11 May 1971, a Dallas restaurateur named Mariano Martinez got tired of his bartenders failing to keep up with demand for margaritas made to his father’s recipe. He had seen a Slurpee machine in a 7-Eleven, bought a soft-serve ice cream machine, modified it, pulled the lever, and produced the world’s first frozen margarita. He never patented it. Every frozen margarita machine on earth descends from that one.

The original is now in the Smithsonian’s National Museum of American History, which lists it among the museum’s notable inventions. This is why Dallas calls itself the official home of the frozen margarita — and it is a real claim, not a marketing one. Mariano’s Hacienda Ranch in Arlington, two miles from the stadium and open from 9am Sunday, carries the name.

Margaritas & More

Two million pounds of hand-carved ice held at 9°F, with parkas provided. Ten themed rooms and six two-storey ice slides, carved by sculptors who travel in from Harbin. This year’s theme is Harry Potter and opening night is the Friday the first two arrive. Tickets from around $33.
